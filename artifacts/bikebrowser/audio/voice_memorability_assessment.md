# Voice Memorability Assessment (Executive Brain) — Step 7

The test, per character: *"If a player closes the game tomorrow, what will they
remember this character **sounded** like?"* Scored **before** (browser-TTS fallback,
the shipped reality until this turn) and **target** (after the XTTS mapping fix +
audition). Weak answers get a why + the fix.

| Character | Remembered-as (BEFORE, browser TTS) | Strength | Target (after fix + audition) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zuzu** | "the default narrator-ish kid voice" — indistinct | **Weak** | A bright, steady, hopeful kid you'd recognize — clarity is her signature |
| **Dex** | "a faster, higher robot" — energy without a person | **Weak** | The fast, brash kid whose voice *cracks* when he's sincere — the crack is the memory |
| **Mr. Chen** | "calm older man" — passable but generic | **Medium** | The unhurried, warm mentor; the voice you trust |
| **Mrs. Ramirez** | "a woman from the block" — generic | **Weak–Medium** | Warm, protective, community — the relief in her Crossing line |
| **Auntie Mariam** | "an older woman" — Arabic flavor absent on fallback | **Weak** | Quiet, patient, wise — Arabic warmth; the seeds line |

## Why the BEFORE answers were weak
All five rendered on **browser Web Speech**, which has no character identity — only
pitch/rate. So the *most memorable thing about each voice was nothing*: players would
remember **lines** (Zuzu's crossing reflection, Dex's "Brain"), not **voices**. For a
game whose thesis is "remember these people," that is a real gap — the writing carries
memorability the audio doesn't yet support.

## Highest-leverage memory hooks (one per character)
Bake/audition these first — each is the single line most likely to *become* the
character's remembered sound:
- **Dex:** the crack — *"...okay. Okay, it held. Every plank."* (cocky→sincere in one breath)
- **Zuzu:** the reflection — *"I thought I was fixing a bridge. Maybe I was building a way for people to come back to each other."*
- **Mr. Chen:** quiet evidence-framing in his heart scene (warm, sure).
- **Mrs. Ramirez:** the Crossing relief — *"...I can breathe again."*
- **Auntie Mariam:** the seeds — *"My garden can grow on both sides now... Shukran, Zuzu."* (in Arabic-flavored warmth)

## Verdict
Pre-fix memorability: **carried entirely by the writing, not the voices** — a weak spot
for a relationship-first game. The mapping fix converts five generic browser voices
into five distinct neural ones; the **audition of the five hook lines above** is what
turns "distinct" into "memorable." That audition is the highest-impact audio action
remaining.
