# Skate Park — Visual Bible (Executive Brain) — Phase 1

**Mandatory before any production art.** Defines the Skate Park's look so it becomes
one of Act 1's most memorable places. Governance anchor: this **must stay inside the
established style** — pixel-JRPG / anime-adjacent, SNES/GBA-era, NOT photorealism or
cel-anime (arc.md visual_bible + decision #2). The directive's inspirations are
**mood references, not render targets**.

## Reconciling the inspirations (mood, rendered in pixel)
| Inspiration | What we take | What we do NOT take |
|---|---|---|
| Zelda TotK / BotW | sense of a *place you want to explore*; readable silhouettes; "build-your-own-fun" | 3D, realistic lighting |
| Studio Ghibli | warm, lived-in environments; nature reclaiming structures; soft golden light | painterly film render |
| Jet Set Radio | bold flat color blocks, graffiti/murals, energy, motion lines | cel-shaded 3D, urban grime realism |
| Tony Hawk's Pro Skater | line-and-flow legibility; ramp/obstacle vocabulary | sports-sim HUD, photoreal skaters |
| No Man's Sky | a horizon that invites; landmark-on-the-hill draw | procedural realism |
| Pixar | environmental storytelling — props that imply history | 3D render |

**Synthesis:** a *warm, hand-built desert skate park* in BikeBrowser's pixel style —
saguaro-and-mesquite framing (Sonoran continuity with the neighborhood), bold flat
color + murals (Jet Set energy), golden-hour lighting, and clear ramp silhouettes a
child reads instantly. **Educational Adventure, not Sports Simulation.**

## Target emotional response
Excitement · wonder · creativity · ownership · exploration. **Players should want to
visit even with no active quest** — the park is a draw (a landmark on the rise past
the repaired bridge), not a menu.

## Environmental storytelling (read the neighborhood by looking)
The park *shows* its history: repaired structures next to cracked ones; an old
half-finished ramp ("abandoned project"); community murals (who painted them?); local
vegetation pushing through; a hand-lettered "Zuzu helped fix this" plaque after the
player contributes; small educational installations (a painted trajectory arc on a
wall, a "this ramp = 30°" sign). Every prop earns its place by implying a story or a
physics idea.

## Visual evolution (every upgrade must be unmistakable at a glance)
| Level | Read in one glance | Signature props |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Partially damaged** | "this place needs help" | missing rails, cracked surfaces, temporary plywood ramps, weeds |
| **2 — Repaired** | "it's alive again" | new clean ramps, active rider NPCs, swept ground, fresh paint |
| **3 — Community-built** | "people love this place" | murals, planters/landscaping, string lights, donated artwork |
| **4 — Engineering park** | "this is a laboratory" | modular bolt-together obstacles, experimental shapes, visible physics installations (pendulum, ramp-angle gauge) |
| **5 — Regional showcase** | "this is THE place" | iconic centerpiece (a signature bowl/spine), banners, a crowd, destination-quality skyline silhouette |

The same park footprint persists; the *layers* accrete — so returning players see
their impact (ownership).

## Palette & lighting
- Warm Sonoran base (sand, terracotta, dusty teal sky) continuous with the
  neighborhood; ramps in honest material colors (concrete gray, steel cool-gray,
  plywood tan, composite charcoal) reusing the UTM material palette (cross-system
  consistency — a steel rail *looks* like the steel the player tested).
- Golden-hour key light; long soft shadows for depth; mural accents in saturated
  Jet-Set primaries used sparingly so they pop.

## Style guardrails (non-negotiable)
- Pixel-JRPG / anime-adjacent only. No photorealism, no cel-anime, no 3D.
- Readability beats realism (arc.md §11): a child must read each ramp's shape, angle,
  and material at a glance — shapes carry the physics lesson.
- Material colors must match the UTM/Construction palette (carry-forward visual
  contract).
- Cultural specifics (mural content) require a human design brief (governance).

## Asset production pipeline (gate, not suggestion)
`Concept → Visual Review → Educational Review → Gameplay Review → Production Asset`.
Each asset must pass **readability · educational value · visual consistency · style
consistency · visual appeal** before approval.

**Visual-Truth gate = HUMAN review.** This environment has no local multimodal art
critique (standing governance: art quality = Human). Therefore:
- I can produce: this bible, concept descriptions, and **procedural placeholder** park
  geometry/obstacles (Phaser primitives, no painted assets) for the prototype.
- Iconic production art (painted backdrop, murals, signature centerpiece) is
  **human-gated** via a `replacement_proposal` + review — same rule as the
  Crossing/Dex art. No production art ships before that review passes.

## Memorability checklist (the bar)
A playtester, the day after, should be able to say: *"the skate park — the one on the
hill past the bridge, with the murals and the big bowl, where you could see your jump
arc."* If that sentence isn't earned by the visuals, the bible has failed and we
iterate before production.
