# Educational Progression Framework — Grades 2–4

Expands the Grade 2→3→4 skeleton (curriculum-map §2) into a full
framework. **Discipline (audit R1/R2/R3):** these grades and standards
are an **invisible coverage check on emergent gameplay** — the player
learns through interaction, never worksheets. Every "example quest" below
is an in-world problem; the grade label is developer/parent-facing only.
Progression is **demonstrated-mastery driven** (you advance by predicting
correctly), not age-locked.

The framework crosses two axes: **grade band** (cognitive readiness) ×
**Educational Hierarchy** (Observe → Explain → Predict → Engineer →
Teach). A grade is not a wall — a Grade-2 player who predicts well moves
up; a Grade-4 concept can appear early as a guided Observe.

---

## Grade 2 — Concrete, hands-on (dominant: Observe → first Explain)

**Expected capabilities**
- Notice and name parts and living things; count and measure with simple
  units; recognize patterns; follow a 1–2 step cause→effect.

**Hierarchy emphasis:** Observe (primary), Explain (emerging).

**Reasoning domains:** pattern_recognition, spatial_reasoning,
estimation (counting/measuring), language_reasoning (follow simple NPC
instructions).

**Biological domains:** Ecology (name plants/animals, habitats),
Ethnobotany (a plant is useful).

**Engineering domains:** mechanical (bike parts), construction (simple
structure), navigation (read a local map).

**Example quests (in-world):**
- *"Find five plants near the wash and name what makes each different."*
  (Observe; pattern_recognition) → links forward to a Grade-3 "why" quest.
- *"Count the bike's gears and measure which is biggest."* (Observe,
  estimation; mechanical)
- *"Which way is the broken bridge — read the map."* (spatial; navigation)

---

## Grade 3 — First abstraction (dominant: Explain → first Predict)

**Expected capabilities**
- Say *why* (friction, simple circuits, "extraction changes the result");
  multiplication/area/perimeter and fractions **as needed by a build**;
  multi-step cause→effect; control one variable in a fair test.

**Hierarchy emphasis:** Explain (primary), Predict (emerging).

**Reasoning domains:** causal_reasoning, experimental_design,
estimation, logical_deduction.

**Biological domains:** Ethnobotany (process a plant into a material),
Phytochemistry preview (why boiling ≠ alcohol extraction), Ecology
(food-chain links).

**Engineering domains:** electrical (e-bike circuit), combustion preview
(charcoal/fuel), materials (fiber strength), construction (load).

**Example quests:**
- *"Why did the yucca cord hold but the dry-grass cord snapped?"*
  (Explain; causal + experimental_design; materials/ethnobotany)
- *"Lay out the bridge deck — how many planks of this length fit?"*
  (area/perimeter as a build problem; estimation; construction)
- *"Boil vs soak the prickly-pear fruit — which gives more dye?"*
  (Explain→Predict; experimental_design; phytochemistry preview)

---

## Grade 4 — Systems and prediction (dominant: Predict → first Engineer)

**Expected capabilities**
- Predict outcomes before acting; optimize under constraints; reason
  about variables and multi-part systems; first electricity/chemistry as
  systems; read feedback.

**Hierarchy emphasis:** Predict (primary), Engineer (emerging), Teach
(occasional — explain to an NPC).

**Reasoning domains:** systems_thinking, optimization, causal_reasoning,
experimental_design, logical_deduction.

**Biological domains:** Cellular preview (microscope), Ecology systems
(remove a species → predict), Pharmacology preview (dose-response).

**Engineering domains:** systems integration (car), fluid/aero preview,
materials optimization (strength-to-weight).

**Example quests:**
- *"Predict the density/strength tradeoff, then choose the wood for the
  tool."* (Predict→Engineer; optimization; materials → seeds Ch6)
- *"If the yucca moth disappears, what happens to the yucca? Predict,
  then check."* (Predict; systems_thinking; ecology → seeds Ch7)
- *"Teach Mr. Chen why the wider tire grips better."* (Teach; causal;
  mechanical)

## Coverage & standards (developer/parent-facing only)

| Grade | Arizona floor (illustrative; specialist pass pending) | Hierarchy center | Reasoning growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | measurement, counting, geometry basics, habitat science, map reading | Observe | pattern, spatial, estimation |
| 3 | multiplication, area/perimeter, fractions, life cycles, simple forces | Explain | causal, experimental_design |
| 4 | variables, multi-step reasoning, systems, electricity intro | Predict | systems_thinking, optimization |

Rules (from arc.md §2 / reasoning-substrate):
- Arizona is the **required floor**; advanced-state (MA/CA/VA/Singapore)
  and gifted (Beast/AoPS/Mensa) content is **additive enrichment**, never
  gating.
- Coverage is **reported, not shown to the player** (no visible grades).
- Every Observe quest links forward (no dead-end facts).
- Predict-and-above is the **majority** of quests by Grade 3+.

## How this stays a game (audit R1–R3 enforcement)
- No worksheet, quiz, or "Grade N" label is ever shown in-game.
- Standards are a *coverage lens applied after* a quest is designed as a
  fun in-world problem — never the quest's prompt.
- Enrichment/gifted = *harder versions of the same in-world problem*
  (Mensa-style "can you figure it out?"), surfaced via reasoning tags.
