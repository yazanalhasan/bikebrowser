## First Impression

The game communicates its systems well, but the child-facing emotional read is still too flat. The player can tell what to do because the HUD and prompts are clear, not because the world itself naturally invites curiosity. The scene is stable and readable enough to play, but it still feels like a labeled systems map rather than a place a child wants to enter.

## Strongest Moments

- The bridge and dry wash are the strongest world motif. Even in placeholder form, the broken crossing clearly tells the player "something happened here."
- The notebook is understandable and reinforces evidence gathering.
- The material table, UTM, and garage cluster form a useful gameplay triangle.
- The trust line "Trust grew because Zuzu showed evidence, not guesses" is emotionally and educationally strong.
- The wider map clue is restrained and does not overexpose future acts.
- Interaction prompts are visible and consistently positioned near actionable objects.

## Weakest Moments

- The road dominates the scene. It visually reads as the most important object even though it is mostly traversal space.
- The top-left quest HUD and bottom-right evidence HUD make the scene feel debug-heavy. A child sees boxes before seeing the neighborhood.
- NPCs are readable as interactable circles, but not memorable as people. Their identity comes from labels rather than posture, staging, or color.
- The garage/workbench is mechanically important but not yet emotionally iconic. It should feel like the warm creative heart of Act 1.
- The player sprite is small and sometimes competes with interaction rings and nearby NPC rings.
- The notebook panel is legible but text-heavy. It feels useful, not treasured.
- The bridge repair state is clear, but not quite satisfying. The "other side" does not yet feel like a place the child wants to reach.

## Confusion Zones

- NPC cluster at the start: Mr. Chen, Mrs. Ramirez, Auntie Mariam, bike, player, and prompt rings overlap visually. A first-time child may not know whether to inspect the bike, talk, or follow the bike icon.
- Garage cluster: UTM, chemistry, materials table, garage label, and workbench label sit close together. It is mechanically efficient but visually busy.
- Bridge/dry wash: the player understands "bridge problem," but the sign, map gate, flood line, dry wash path label, and cactus marker all compete.
- Notebook counts are useful for developers but may be too abstract for a child. "Material 3/4" is less inviting than "3 material clues."

## Emotional Flatness Zones

- Zuzu home and garage are simple silhouettes without enough warmth cues. They mark locations but do not yet suggest family, safety, or creativity.
- The street feels empty and horizontal. It lacks gentle rhythm changes that would make exploration feel like moving through a real neighborhood.
- Ecology currently reads as a plant interaction station rather than a small living desert patch.
- Chemistry reads as a functional station, not yet as a little experiment with anticipation.

## Clutter Zones

- Start area: NPC rings, labels, prompt, bike, HUD, and player stack in a narrow band.
- Garage/material area: interactive rings are close together, creating "bubble noise."
- Bottom-right debug/evidence panel is dense and competes with the dry wash and bridge.

## Interaction Ambiguity

- The prompt text is clear, but there are moments where several interaction rings overlap and the prompt alone carries too much responsibility.
- Some labels are partially occluded or crowded, especially around the garage/workbench and NPC cluster.
- The bridge sign prompt appears clearly, but the bridge itself could pull the eye more strongly than the sign.

## Landmark Readability

Strongest landmarks:

- Broken bridge / dry wash
- Garage/workbench cluster
- Zuzu home silhouette
- Materials table

Weakest landmarks:

- Ecology patch: needs stronger "desert helper" identity without adding clutter.
- Wider map gate: readable prompt, but the gate could feel more mysterious and celebratory.
- NPC area: readable labels, weak personalities.

## NPC Readability Findings

- NPC colors help slightly, but silhouettes are too similar.
- NPCs need small identity cues: stance, color accent, or item association.
- Mrs. Ramirez and Auntie Mariam need more spatial breathing room.
- Voice identity has improved at the system layer, but visual identity has not caught up yet.

## Progression Readability Findings

- The objective list makes progression clear.
- Evidence feedback is clear but compact and debug-like.
- Notebook unlocks work, but the visual reward should feel more like discovering a clue.
- Material testing produces a clear conclusion, but the child-facing deformation/comparison is still modest.
- Bridge completion is understandable, but the emotional payoff could be stronger with a clearer crossing cue and less HUD competition.

## Initial Refinement Targets

1. Reduce road dominance through palette/contrast, not by adding detail.
2. Make the garage/workbench warmer and more iconic.
3. Give the bridge/dry wash a stronger focal silhouette and "want to cross" composition.
4. Separate NPCs slightly and give each a small visual identity cue.
5. Improve Zuzu visibility against rings and road colors.
6. Soften debug-heavy HUD presentation for child-facing play while preserving test hooks.
7. Make notebook unlocks feel more like field discoveries and less like checklist accounting.
8. Increase scene warmth with subtle lighting bands and purposeful landmark contrast.
