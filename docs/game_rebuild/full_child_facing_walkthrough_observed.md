## What A Child Sees First

The first read is a quiet neighborhood with a big road, a house, clustered people, a bike, a garage, a bridge/wash area, and clear HUD instructions. The game is immediately controllable and not visually chaotic.

However, the eye still goes to UI boxes and road bands before it goes to the emotional premise. The child understands "there is a task," but does not yet strongly feel "I am in Zuzu's neighborhood and something happened to this place."

## What A Child Understands Without Text

Understood visually:

- The bike is important.
- The garage/workbench area is important.
- The bridge/wash area is a problem.
- NPCs can probably be talked to.
- There is a path toward the right side of the world.

Not yet understood without text:

- Why each NPC matters.
- Why the garage is emotionally special.
- What the UTM physically does.
- Why chemistry matters to bridge repair.
- Why the ecology patch is more than a plant station.
- What makes the notebook rewarding.

## What Feels Warm

- The dusk palette and garage glow help.
- Zuzu's home and garage placement give a local feeling.
- Mrs. Ramirez/Mr. Chen/Auntie Mariam clustered near the starting area implies community.
- Dialogue and feedback language are warm and evidence-oriented.

## What Feels Confusing

- The starting cluster has many interactables close together.
- The objective HUD is clear but dense.
- The clue panel feels useful but still like a debug/status box.
- The garage cluster has too many mechanic/tool stations close together for a first glance.
- The notebook has improved language but remains visually text-first.

## What Feels Like A Real Place

- The dry wash, bridge, flood line, and path cues are the most place-like.
- The home/garage/street layout is coherent.
- Sparse desert plants and dusk color support Sonoran mood.

## What Still Feels Like Systems Connected By Code

- HUD panels expose state more than they dramatize experience.
- UTM/material testing is primarily a command/result loop.
- Chemistry and ecology are station interactions rather than embodied situations.
- Notebook is a structured list rather than an artifact Zuzu owns.
- NPC visual identities come from labels and cues more than from character art.

## Emotionally Flat Moments

- Opening scene: playable and clear, but not yet magical or memorable.
- First garage use: mechanically important, visually still modest.
- UTM testing: informative, but not tactile enough.
- Chemistry: useful result, low visual anticipation.
- Bridge repair: successful, but the before/after payoff is understated.

## Too Debug-Like Screens

- Opening state: objective HUD and clue panel frame the experience as a task board.
- Material testing: clue panel communicates evidence well but looks like a developer status readout.
- Notebook: readable, but mostly text/counts.

## Where The World Fails To Guide Curiosity

- The garage does not yet invite the child through visual storytelling alone.
- The ecology patch does not yet look like a small living habitat.
- Chemistry does not yet create anticipation through color/change/timing.
- NPCs do not visually suggest their roles strongly enough.
- The wider map gate is clearer now, but still needs a field-map/reveal moment.

## Current Verdict

Act 1 is a strong playable substrate with improving warmth. It is not yet a complete child-facing production slice. The next work must turn the highest-value placeholders into authored, sparse, runtime-safe production art and reduce the normal-play HUD's debug energy.
