# Tire Repair Reference Analysis

Date: 2026-05-22
Reference: REI Expert Advice, "How to Fix a Flat Bike Tire"

## Use Boundary

The REI article was used as reference grammar only: repair sequence, staging logic, cause/effect clarity, and tool readability. No layouts, wording, photography, or visual assets were copied.

## Extracted Repair Grammar

The reference presents a flat tire as a sequence of mechanical access, diagnosis, repair, and verification:

1. Remove/access the wheel.
2. Deflate and unseat the tire/tube.
3. Find the cause of the flat by inspecting tire, tube, valve, and rim.
4. Patch or replace the tube.
5. Reinstall/reinflate/verify.

The most useful teaching move is that diagnosis happens before repair. The article names the cause-finding step explicitly: inspect the tire and tube for punctures, cuts, or tears, and check matching locations across tire/tube/rim. It also gives small-leak localization patterns: feel/listen for escaping air or use bubbles in water. The patching sequence is likewise causal: find damage, clean/dry it, roughen the surface, apply adhesive, then press one patch over the damaged area.

## Lessons For BikeBrowser

### Visual Communication

The repair should be staged as a mechanic's work surface, not a scattered inventory checklist. The player needs one central object: wheel/tube. Tools orbit the object by job:

- left/near hand: find leak
- center: hole/prepared surface/patch
- side: pump and gauge
- corner: patch kit as supply, not the applied patch

### Leak Localization

The leak must be visible as a cause, not just a quest step. Use:

- tiny dark puncture mark
- air/bubble trace leaving the hole
- highlight at one fixed location
- notebook note that records the leak zone

### Patch Distinction

The patch kit is a supply prop. The applied patch is a single rubber object. It must sit directly over the puncture and remain anchored there during the repair.

### Cause And Effect

Every stage should change the same visible system:

- leak open: air trace visible, tire soft
- tube exposed: hole can be worked on
- surface prepared: scuffed/glue zone appears around hole
- patch pressed: air trace fades
- pump: sidewall rounds out and gauge rises
- spin check: wheel only spins after the tire holds air

### Child-Readable Insight

The target sentence is:

> Air escaped through a tiny hole. A cleaned patch covered the hole, so the tire could hold air again.

That is the notebook truth the scene should teach.

## Source Notes

Relevant reference points from the article:

- It summarizes five basic flat-fix steps: remove wheel, remove tube, find cause, patch/replace, reinstall.
- It describes inspecting tire/tube/valve/rim to avoid repeating the same flat.
- It describes locating small tube leaks by feeling/listening for air or watching for bubbles.
- It describes patch prep as clean/dry, roughen, adhesive, press patch.
